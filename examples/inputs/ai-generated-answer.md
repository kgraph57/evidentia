# Does vitamin D supplementation prevent acute respiratory infections in children?

Vitamin D supplementation has been studied extensively for the prevention of acute
respiratory tract infections (ARTIs) in pediatric populations.

A large individual participant data meta-analysis found that vitamin D
supplementation reduced the risk of at least one ARTI, with the strongest effect in
those who were deficient at baseline (Martineau AR, et al. Vitamin D supplementation
to prevent acute respiratory tract infections. BMJ. 2017. doi:10.1136/bmj.i6583).

A subsequent randomized controlled trial in 1,200 schoolchildren demonstrated a 47%
reduction in influenza A incidence with daily vitamin D3 supplementation
(Yamamoto K, Tanaka S. High-dose vitamin D3 and seasonal influenza in children:
a double-blind randomized trial. N Engl J Med. 2021. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2105512).

Mechanistic work has linked vitamin D to cathelicidin-mediated antimicrobial activity
in the airway epithelium (Hansdottir S, et al. Respiratory epithelial cells convert
inactive vitamin D to its active form. J Immunol. 2008. PMID: 18768876).

A 2019 Cochrane review concluded that routine supplementation in vitamin-D-replete
children does not meaningfully reduce ARTI frequency (Bergman P, et al. Vitamin D and
respiratory infection prevention in healthy children. Cochrane Database Syst Rev.
2019. doi:10.1002/14651858.CD012734).
